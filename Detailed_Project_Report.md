### Calculation Explanation

Fixed Assets = Net Block + CWIP<br/>
Net Block = (Property, Plant & Equipment) = Rs. 7,51,087 Cr. [FY 2025-2026]<br/>
CWIP = (Capital Work in Progress) = Rs. 1,92,287 Cr. [FY 2025-2026]<br/>
Cash & Other Assets = Total Assets - (Fixed Assets + Inventory + Debtors)<br/>

Total Debt = (Borrowings) = Non-current Liabilities Borrowings + Current Liabilities Borrowings<br/>
Other Liabilities = Total Equity & Liabilities - (Total Debt + Total Equity)<br/>

EBITDA = (Earnings Before Interest, Depreciation & Tax) = Revenue from Operations - (Material Cost + Employee Benefit Expenses + Excersise Duty + Other Expense)<br/>
Total Material Cost = Cost of Materials Consumed + Purchase of Stock-in-Trade + Changes in Inventories of Finished Goods, Work-in-Progress and Stock-in-Trade = (4,43,101 + 2,55,829 + (-10,267)) = Rs. 6,88,663 Cr.<br/>

Actual No. of Shares = 13532 Capital/ 10 face value = 1353.2<br/>

## Insights

**1) Profitability Ratios**<br/>

**1.1) Profit Margin**<br/>

Profit Margin = Net Profit ÷ Revenue × 100

- FY 2024–25: 8.24%
- FY 2025–26: 8.88%
- Change: +0.64 percentage points<br/>

![](assets/%235Chart2.png)

The improvement indicates that the company generated approximately ₹8.88 of net profit for every ₹100 of revenue in FY 2025–26, compared with ₹8.24 in the previous year. This represents a meaningful improvement in the proportion of revenue retained as profit.

**Management's comment:**<br/>
Management described FY 2025–26 as a strong financial year, with consolidated revenue growing 9.8% and PAT increasing 17.8%. It attributed the performance to the diversified business portfolio, operational agility and robust Indian market demand. Digital Services, Retail and Media & Entertainment were among the businesses supporting the overall performance.

**Analyst insight:**\
The improvement in the calculated margin is encouraging because profit grew faster than revenue. It suggests better overall earnings conversion during the year. The trend also provides some comfort that the company's diversified business model is helping support profitability despite a challenging global operating environment.

**1.2) Return on Assets (ROA)**

ROA = Net Profit ÷ Total Assets × 100

- FY 2024–25: 4.14%
- FY 2025–26: 4.38%
- Change: +0.24 percentage points

ROA improved modestly, indicating that the company generated a somewhat higher level of profit from its asset base. Total assets increased from ₹19,50,121 crore to ₹21,78,140 crore, while PAT increased at a faster rate.

**Management's comment:**\
Management highlighted strong internal cash-flow generation and disciplined capital allocation. Capital expenditure increased to ₹1,44,271 crore in FY 2025–26 and was principally directed towards growth projects in O2C and New Energy, together with continuing investments in Digital Services and Retail.

**Analyst insight:**\
The improvement in ROA is positive, particularly given the substantial increase in the asset base. It suggests that the additional capital deployed during the year was beginning to translate into earnings. However, the relatively small improvement also indicates that returns from the expanded asset base should be monitored for major growth as projects mature.

**1.3) Return on Equity (ROE)**

ROE = Net Profit ÷ Shareholders' Equity × 100

- FY 2024–25: 8.00%
- FY 2025–26: 8.80%
- Change: +0.80 percentage points

ROE increased by 0.80 percentage points, showing that the company generated a better return on shareholders' funds during FY 2025–26. PAT increased from ₹80,787 crore to ₹95,610 crore, while equity increased from ₹10,09,626 crore to ₹10,85,866 crore.

**Management's comment:**\
Management reported that Return on Net Worth improved to 10.1% in FY 2025–26 from 8.2% in the previous year, primarily due to the increase in net profit. The company notes that this measure is adjusted for CWIP and revaluation.

**Analyst insight:**\
The improvement in the supplied ROE calculation is a favourable development for shareholders. Importantly, the higher return appears to have been supported by stronger profitability rather than a significant increase in financial leverage, as the Debt-Equity ratio remained broadly stable. This makes the improvement in shareholder returns relatively reassuring.

**2) Efficiency Ratios**

**2.1) Debtors Turnover**

Debtors Turnover = Revenue ÷ Average Debtors

- FY 2024–25: 23.26 times
- FY 2025–26: 18.39 times
- Change: -4.87 times

The ratio declined marginally. This means that receivables were converted into revenue at a slower rate than in the previous year. Debtors increased from ₹42,121 crore to ₹58,491 crore, while revenue increased at a comparatively slower pace.

![](assets/%236Chart3.png)

**Management's comment:**\
Management stated that the Group uses diverse trade-financing and structured products to optimise working capital and maintains sufficient liquidity and undrawn credit lines to meet short-term obligations.

**Analyst insight:**\
This is one of the areas requiring attention. The decline does not necessarily indicate a deterioration in credit quality, as it can also reflect changes in business mix, sales terms or the timing of collections. Nevertheless, the faster increase in receivables means that disciplined collection and working-capital management will remain important as the business expands.

**2.2) Asset Turnover**

Asset Turnover = Revenue ÷ Total Assets

- FY 2024–25: 0.50 times
- FY 2025–26: 0.49 times
- Change: -0.01 times

The ratio remained broadly stable but declined slightly.

**Management's comment:**\
Management highlighted substantial capital expenditure of ₹1,44,271 crore, with investment focused on O2C, New Energy, Digital Services and Retail. The company indicated that capital expenditure requirements were adequately funded by cash profits.

**Analyst insight:**\
The marginal decline should be viewed in the context of the company's expansion programme. New investments may take time to reach full operating capacity, so a temporary moderation in asset turnover is understandable. The key consideration going forward will be whether these investments generate sufficient incremental revenue and earnings as they mature.

**2.3) Inventory Turnover**

Inventory Turnover = Material Cost ÷ Average Inventory

- FY 2024–25: 4.31 times
- FY 2025–26: 4.12 times
- Change: -0.19 times

Inventory turnover declined moderately, indicating that inventory moved somewhat more slowly relative to material costs. Inventory increased from ₹1,46,062 crore to ₹1,66,941 crore.

**Management's comment:**\
Management described the O2C business as having maintained operational excellence in volatile energy markets and highlighted higher operating rates, optimised feedstock sourcing and higher domestic product placements as factors supporting profitability.

**Analyst insight:**\
The decline is moderate and should be interpreted alongside the nature of the company's businesses, particularly energy and manufacturing operations where inventory requirements can be substantial. Also, the current year's global disruption in the fuel industry should be taken into consideration. Still, the trend suggests that inventory efficiency deserves continued monitoring. Effective inventory planning can help preserve cash generation while supporting uninterrupted operations.

**3) Valuation Ratios**

The management discussion reviewed for this analysis does not provide a specific commentary on the P/E ratio or the market valuation multiple. Management does, however, emphasise its commitment to enhancing shareholder value through diversified businesses, resilient cash flows and a robust balance sheet.

**Analyst insight:**\
The lower P/E ratio, when considered alongside stronger profitability, is reasonably constructive from an earnings perspective. It may suggest that earnings growth has outpaced the valuation multiple. However, valuation ratios are influenced by market expectations, share-price movements and investor sentiment, so the change should not be interpreted independently of broader market conditions.

- FY 2025–26: 0.40%
- Change: +0.01 percentage points

Dividend yield remained broadly stable, with a very small improvement.

**Management's comment:**\
Management's discussion does not provide a specific explanation of the calculated dividend yield. It does state that enhancing shareholder value remains a key objective and that capital allocation discipline is an important part of the company's financial strategy.

**Analyst insight:**\
The stable dividend yield indicates continuity in the company's shareholder distribution profile. The slight increase is positive but not material enough to represent a major change in dividend characteristics. Investors would therefore likely consider dividend yield together with earnings growth and the company's long-term investment requirements.

**4) Solvency Ratios**

**4.1) Debt-Equity Ratio**

Debt-Equity Ratio = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity

- FY 2024–25: 0.34
- FY 2025–26: 0.34
- Change: 0.0

The ratio remained unchanged. For every ₹1 of shareholders' equity, the company had approximately ₹0.34 of debt in FY 2025–26.

Management described the balance sheet as robust and highlighted prudent leverage management. Gross debt at March 31, 2026 was ₹3,74,421 crore, while net debt was ₹1,24,717 crore. Management also stated that capital expenditure was adequately funded by cash profits.

The broadly stable Debt-Equity ratio is reassuring. **4.2) Interest Earned / Interest Coverage**

Interest Coverage = EBITDA ÷ Interest Expense

- FY 2024–25: 6.81 times
- FY 2025–26: 6.612 times
- Change: -0.198 times

The ratio declined slightly, meaning that EBITDA provided approximately 6.61 times coverage of finance costs in FY 2025–26, compared with 6.81 times previously.

**Management's comment:**\
Management's officially reported Interest Coverage Ratio, calculated using its own methodology, increased from 5.59 to 8.83 because of higher operating profit and lower finance costs. Management also highlighted prudent leverage management and strong internal cash-flow generation.

**Analyst insight:**\
The supplied simplified ratio shows a small decline, but the coverage level remains strong at more than six times. The difference from management's reported ratio demonstrates the importance of methodology when comparing financial ratios. Overall, the company's reported coverage position provides reasonable comfort regarding its ability to service finance costs.
**5) Liquidity Ratios**

**5.1) Current Ratio**

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

- FY 2024–25: 1.10
- FY 2025–26: 1.09
- Change: -0.01

The current ratio remained close to 1.0, with only a marginal decline. This indicates that current assets were approximately 1.09 times current liabilities in FY 2025–26.

**Management's comment:**\
Management stated that Reliance prioritises liquidity management by maintaining strong cash reserves, sufficient undrawn credit lines and diversified financing arrangements to meet short-term obligations.

**Analyst insight:**\
Even though the standard rule of thumb for Current Ratio is 1.33:1, it is essential to note that the Asset expansion was funded by Cash Profits. Importantly, the ratio should not be viewed in isolation: management's emphasis on cash reserves and undrawn credit facilities provides additional liquidity support beyond the simplified current-ratio calculation.

**5.2) Quick Ratio**

Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

- FY 2024–25: 0.778
- FY 2025–26: 0.789
- Change: +0.011

The quick ratio improved slightly, despite the small decline in the current ratio. This indicates a modest improvement in the company's ability to meet short-term obligations using relatively liquid assets without relying on the sale of inventory.

**Management's comment:**\
Management emphasised maintaining strong cash reserves and sufficient undrawn credit lines, while also using trade financing and structured products to optimise working capital.

**Analyst insight:**\
Even though the standard rule of thumb for Quick Ratio is 1:1, it is interesting to note that the Quick Ratio follows an increasing trend rather than the Current Ratio. It suggests that the company's immediately available liquidity position has remained resilient. Although the ratio remains below 1.0, management's broader liquidity framework—including cash reserves and available credit facilities—provides useful context and supports the overall assessment of financial flexibility.

## Overall Analyst Assessment

The FY 2025–26 financial picture is broadly positive, with the strongest improvement visible in profitability and shareholder returns. Profit Margin increased from 8.24% to 8.88%, ROA improved from 4.14% to 4.38%, and ROE increased from 8.00% to 8.80%. These movements are consistent with the underlying growth in PAT from ₹80,787 crore to ₹95,610 crore.

The efficiency ratios present a more mixed picture. Debtors Turnover declined considerably, while Inventory Turnover and Asset Turnover also softened. This does not necessarily signal weakness, particularly given the company's significant investment programme and expansion across several businesses, but it does highlight the importance of effective working-capital and asset-utilisation management.

The solvency position remains reassuring. The Debt-Equity ratio was essentially unchanged, while the supplied Interest Coverage ratio remained comfortably above six times. Management's own reported Interest Coverage Ratio improved substantially to 8.83, further supporting the view that debt-servicing capacity remains sound.

Liquidity was broadly steady. The Current Ratio moved marginally lower, but the Quick Ratio improved slightly. Management's stated policy of maintaining strong cash reserves, undrawn credit lines and diversified financing arrangements provides an important qualitative complement to these simplified liquidity ratios.

From a valuation perspective, the decline in the P/E ratio from 24.77 to 22.51, alongside stronger earnings, is reasonably constructive. Dividend Yield remained essentially unchanged, suggesting stability in the shareholder distribution profile.

## Conclusion

Overall, FY 2025–26 reflects a year of stronger profitability, improving returns, broadly stable leverage and resilient liquidity, alongside some moderation in operating efficiency. Management's emphasis on diversified growth, disciplined capital allocation, internal cash-flow generation and prudent leverage provides a supportive backdrop to the financial trends.

The principal areas to watch in the coming period are **receivables management, inventory efficiency, asset utilisation and liquidity headroom** as the company continues to undertake significant capital expenditure. At the same time, the improvement in earnings and returns provides a positive foundation for the company's continuing expansion.

In balanced terms, the financial ratios suggest that Reliance Industries entered FY 2026–27 with a stronger profitability profile and a broadly stable financial structure, while the scale of ongoing investment makes disciplined execution and efficient deployment of capital increasingly important.
